# adr-001: to buy or not to buy (to suffer the slings and arrows of building thing syourself), given a pythonic stack.

context:
the microservice architecture is scaling rapidly, and i am approaching the large operational tier (projected 1,000 services, 5 tb of logs per day, and 10 million metric events per second). currently, i rely on datadog for observability and telemetry storage. 

conventional engineering wisdom dictates that one should buy rather than build during early and mid-development phases to conserve engineering cycles. however, my latest financial forecasting model indicates that datadog's pricing structure will cost approximately $620,000.00 per month at this scale. this extreme cost margin makes the buy philosophy (toward early dev phases or startups) financially absurd and has forced me to reconsider the build option.

furthermore, i have direct experience and strong familiarity with a similar, successful (maybe? it was demonstrated to the board in HN just today) project where i utilized flask for web hosting and nats for message transport. i will not go into the details of what this project is about, ha ha. the final stages of that pipeline were designed following strict industry best practices for observability. because i already possess the domain knowledge and familiarity to operate a python/nats stack effectively, the risk of building my own solution is significantly mitigated.

decision:
i will deprecate datadog and build a self-hosted, python-native observability pipeline. i will leverage my familiarity with these systems, using the opentelemetry python sdk (collection), nats jetstream (transport), bytewax (stream processing), and timescaledb (storage). also, from the architecture i have worked on, i would like to introduce security and remote connection via a tailscale mesh network.

consequences:
* positive - massive cost reduction: by utilizing zstd compression in timescaledb and raw cloud block storage, my projected monthly infrastructure cost will drop to $9,322.08. the extreme margin (~98.5% savings) makes the build option undeniably superior despite the engineering time investment. this is based on what i read from timescaledb, which is a flavor of postgres, and as we all know, postgres is very good when it comes to numbers.
* positive - reduced execution risk: leveraging my deep familiarity and prior experience with flask and nats means i am not building this from scratch. i am applying proven best practices from a previous pipeline i built.
* positive - unconstrained metrics: i eliminate arbitrary vendor penalties for custom metric cardinality. the ai models can consume as many unique time-series dimensions as the bare-metal hardware can support.
* negative - operational overhead: i am taking on the burden of managing stateful distributed systems, requiring dedicated engineering time for nats clustering and database maintenance.

alternatives considered:
* datadog with aggressive downsampling: rejected because dropping 80% of data to save money drastically reduced the precision and recall of the payment anomaly detection models.
* self-hosting kafka + elasticsearch: this traditional enterprise route was rejected because kafka introduces heavy jvm tuning overhead. sticking to nats aligns perfectly with my existing familiarity and previous project success.
